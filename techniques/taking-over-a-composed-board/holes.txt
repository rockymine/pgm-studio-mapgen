The board's own negative space, three ways of asking.

### the plan's cells, through the predicate: a void cell with land in all four directions
  330 land cells, 36 hole cells in 4 region(s)
      9 cell(s)  cells x -5..-3 z 8..10   blocks x -20..-8 z 32..44
      9 cell(s)  cells x -4..-2 z -11..-9   blocks x -16..-4 z -44..-32
      9 cell(s)  cells x 1..3 z 8..10   blocks x 4..16 z 32..44
      9 cell(s)  cells x 2..4 z -11..-9   blocks x 8..20 z -44..-32

### the compiler's own answer: one subtract per ENCLOSED void component
    void-1-cut     ring [[-20, 32], [-8, 32], [-8, 44], [-20, 44]]
    void-2-cut     ring [[4, 32], [16, 32], [16, 44], [4, 44]]

### the built world, per variant: how many of each hole's 144 blocks carry ground
  Asked with `transect`, whose `ground` is null on a void column. A render payload answers a
  different question: a crown leaning over the rim and a marker block under it are blocks in that
  column, and a count over them reports a hole as filled that is void to the block.
    1-as-pinned              hole-1   0/144   hole-2   0/144
    2-void-redrawn           hole-1  32/144   hole-2   0/144
    3-a-surface-per-piece    hole-1   0/144   hole-2   0/144
    4-taken-over             hole-1   0/144   hole-2   0/144

`2-void-redrawn` is the only variant with ground inside a hole, and its 32 blocks are the
corners its SUBTRACT was redrawn without. The layout's group mirrors, so the same 32 come out of
hole-1's image at x 8..20, z -44..-32: 64 blocks over the board, and no add was written for any of
them. The board simply states less negative space than it did.

`4-taken-over` took the bar west of hole-1 away, and hole-1 is still void to the block. What
changed is the number of CUTS: with the bar gone that void opens to the sea, so it is no longer
ENCLOSED, the compiler emits one subtract instead of two, and the merged outline states the same
void by tracing round it. A subtract is wanted where a void is enclosed; everywhere else the
outline already says it.
