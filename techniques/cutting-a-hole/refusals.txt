PUT …/sketch, then POST …/sketch/finish — four adds over the same cut in `a-hole`, and what each
of the two calls said about it. The cut is the 20 x 20 subtract at (-176, -156, -72, -52), floor 0.

### an override add at the cut's own floor — the refill
  PUT 200 · finish 422
  SK13 complaint — 'refill' fills 400 column(s) that 'a-hole-cut' takes away — from (-176, -72) — so the
  negative space the board states there is ground in the world. An override add beats a subtract on its
  own layer
  finish: the board cannot be built as drawn

### a plain add at the cut's own floor — the same shape without `override`
  PUT 200 · finish 200
  SK13 complaint — 'plain-refill' draws nothing over 400 column(s) — from (-176, -72) — because
  'a-hole-cut' takes them away, and a subtract beats every plain add on its layer whatever order the two
  are written in. The shape is on the canvas and not in the world

### an add on a second layer, at the cut's own floor
  PUT 200 · finish 422
  SK13 complaint — 'layer-refill' fills 400 column(s) that 'a-hole-cut' takes away — from (-176, -72) — so
  the negative space the board states there is ground in the world. 'layer-refill' is on layer 'other' and
  the subtract on 'ground', and a subtract reaches only the layer it is on
  finish: the board cannot be built as drawn

### an override add one course above the cut's floor — the bridge
  PUT 200 · finish 200
  (nothing said)

The card's own layout is stored again after these, so the map the renders read is the clean one.

`SK13` answers at two volumes. A fill is a **complaint** at the store, which answers 200 and keeps
the shape, and a **refusal** at the finish, which answers 422 and builds nothing — so a board can
sit stored for a long time carrying a fault only the finish says out loud.

A plain add over a cut is the quieter half: it complains at both, the board builds, and that shape
is simply absent from the world.

The floor is the whole of the test. An add standing at or below the subtract's floor puts the
negative space back; the same shape one course higher is a bridge over it and passes in silence.
