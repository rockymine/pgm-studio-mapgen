base_height on a sink shape is the depth in blocks and the floor is the median ground under the
footprint minus it, with a minimum of one. It is clamped where the push is not: the erect pass
writes max(column floor + 1, top) against the COLUMN's floor, which is the plot rectangle's rather
than the sink shape's, so every over-deep sink bottoms out at y1 and stating floor changes nothing.

skirt grades inward and is paid for out of the floor; a push's falloff grades outward and is paid
for out of the mouth.

A relief's `base` is what an unmarked field settles at rather than a rim the marks are cut into, so
one mark takes the whole island with it: the first row is flat at 20 across all 57 stations.

`tread` does nothing at all on an area mark — the last row is the bevel-0 row block for block.

A push's hollow rides the ground it is cut into: its floor falls from 26 at the ring's uphill edge to
14 at its downhill edge, the tilt minus twelve, which is a quarry whose floor drains one way. A sink's
floor is one flat plate at the covered cells' median minus twelve — 20 the whole way across — so its
uphill wall is eighteen deep and its downhill wall six.
An area mark's floor is flat at the h it states and the whole tilt around it is pulled into the
grade that reaches it.
