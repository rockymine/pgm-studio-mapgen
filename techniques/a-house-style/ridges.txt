GET …/column?at=x,z — three columns a house: the ridge line at its middle, the eaves row one block
in from the long wall, and the yard beside it. Every pad solves to the same ground, so the heights
are comparable across the board.

A form is a height and a material before it is a shape. `flat` answers the same course at its
middle and at its eaves, where a `gable` rises between the two and a `gambrel` rises further,
breaking its slope on the way.

`pitch` changes the ridge without changing the form, and `overhang` changes what stands past the
wall without changing the ridge at all.

`shed` is the one form whose high edge is not in the middle — it slopes one way, so the column at
the house's centre is partway up it and the tall side is at a wall.

Across the ridge the two answer the same ridge and the same eaves. Along it they do not: a gable
closes its ends with a wall carried up to the ridge, and a hip slopes them in like the sides. A
roof form read from above, or from one cut, is a form half-read.
