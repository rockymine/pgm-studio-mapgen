# The author's first review of the four experiment boards

Given 2026-10-10 after looking at the renders, and handed back to each builder for one revision round. It is the
author's review, sorted per board; nothing in it is the builders'. The author's general reading is at the end.

## Slatefold, studio (`exp-slatefold-studio`)

1. The board reads a little more coherent than the pgmvox one: there are more things in it.
2. The wool rooms are in an odd spot: on a hill and at the front line. A wool room usually stands at the end of its
   lane, behind the front, not on it.
3. There is a flat stone disc at the front line. If it was meant to be a lake or a pool, it was never filled.

## Slatefold, pgmvox (`exp-slatefold-pgmvox`)

1. Houses with stone roofs and stone infill don't read well. The one house with clay-coloured walls under a stone
   roof (one of the wool rooms) is a really nice house: make that the board's default house style, with some
   variation, so the houses contrast with the stone floors.
2. The houses near the spawn stand all in one line.
3. The bedrock defence wall runs all the way down to the bottom of the world, which looks odd.
4. There is too much stone in general, with no reason for most of it. The stone island of the wool rooms and one
   stone area are fine. The front of the map should be mostly green and hillier, rising toward the settlement, where
   bare stone hills make no sense.
5. Water would add contrast: for example a river running down at the front of the map.
6. A long fence seems to run along the map where it makes no sense.
7. A pine tree is stuck on the roof of a house, probably from the grass patch beside it.
8. The higher wool room, the one with the tower, might be better placed at the front line. (The author is not sure
   it makes sense; keep it where it is if there is a reason, and say what the reason is.)
9. A boulder stands next to an objective.

## Abbeymoor, studio (`exp-abbeymoor-studio`)

1. This one comes a lot closer to a map that reads like a place. The ruin built from layers works, and the houses
   help.
2. The spawn building is somewhere and nowhere at the same time: it does not belong to a place.
3. The terrain is a little clunky.

## Abbeymoor, pgmvox (`exp-abbeymoor-pgmvox`)

1. It really looks like a place, with a nice style and a few flaws. The flower fields are liked, and so is the ruin
   being more intact.
2. The muddy area in the middle is too oval. Make it irregular so it sits in the terrain, and add smaller patches
   of it around the map, especially through the middle, so it feels coherent.
3. Beside the middle, to its left and right, something is missing: perhaps another settlement or a hill (the author
   is unsure a hill fits).
4. The same stone-house problem: use clay for the walls; stone is fine for the base course.
5. Paths should be more pronounced, in dirt, so that stone is kept for where the rock surface shows.
6. A few more trees would be welcome, and some rocks.

## The author's general reading

The capture-the-wool boards came out a lot worse, as expected: their layouts are harder, it is easy to make one
that makes no sense, and it is hard to correct. The pgmvox one is still middling, but its rooms are clearly
separated. A composed layout would help both, but that is not the point of the experiment. The destroy boards are
the closer comparison, and the pgmvox one won the author over.

## Added afterwards

**Abbeymoor, pgmvox, point 7.** An obsidian monument is at most three blocks. A 3 × 3 × 3 obsidian cube is 27 blocks
of obsidian and effectively unbreakable in a match; the studio caps obsidian at three blocks (DC3) for this reason.
