Bunker Cores

Ten ways a core and a wool can depend on each other, drawn as side cuts through a bunker. Hot Dive comes first as the reference: its wool room is sealed behind the core's lava, so a team either leaks the core or dives through the lava with fire resistance. Each idea after it changes one thing: what gets a raider in early, what the leak opens, or how the wool gets out.

The bunkers have no geometry yet. A box is a room, a slab is a door, and the shapes are only there to show what connects to what. Every mechanism is something PGM already reads: the completed and captured filters, triggers with fill and paste-structure actions, dynamic structures, variables, and spawners and portals that take a filter.

Where a core can sit: the leak decides

A core only leaks when lava appears far enough below it, so every design has to give the lava somewhere to fall. PGM (core/Core.java, CoreMatchModule.java) works it out like this:

  1. The casing is every block of the core's material inside the core region, and the lava is every lava block in it. The lava blocks cannot change at all, and the owning team cannot damage the casing.
  2. The leak region is a box under the core: from y 0 up to the core region's lowest y minus leak, reaching 15 blocks past the region's footprint on x and z.
  3. The core leaks the moment any lava block appears inside that box. Flowing lava counts, and so does lava poured from a bucket.

So a core needs an open face the attackers can reach, and below that face a drop that goes past the leak level within 15 blocks of the core. Solid ground under the core and a closed wall around it give the lava nowhere to go. In Hot Dive the floor sits over the void: green's region bottom is y 39 and leak="5", so lava at y 34 or lower leaks it.

The same box punishes mistakes. A lava feature anywhere in it, or a team pouring lava into it, leaks the core, and that is why Hot Dive refuses lava placement in the wool rooms. A wool room under a core sits inside this box.
