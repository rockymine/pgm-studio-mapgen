What the ground is

A grid of heights, and arithmetic over it

The terrain is never sculpted block by block. It is one array H: for each column (x, z), the y of its top block. Each operation takes the whole array and returns a new one, so a volcano is a short list of operations applied in order. Only at the very end is every column filled with blocks up to its height.

Three ways of combining two shapes do almost all the work. max(H, form) only ever lifts: a hill can grow out of the ground but never dig into it. min(H, form) only ever cuts, so a crater can never build anything up. smooth_max and smooth_min do the same with the seam rounded over k blocks, which is why the cone has a foot instead of standing on the plain like a plinth. Because each operation only lifts or only cuts, they can be stacked in almost any order without undoing each other.

Ingredient one

Noise: random numbers, smoothed and stacked

Anything that looks natural starts as fractal noise (noise.fbm). Throw random numbers onto a coarse lattice, here one every 90 blocks, and interpolate smoothly between them. That gives one slow swell. Then do it again with the lattice half as wide and add it at half the strength, and again, and again. Each octave adds detail at half the size and half the height.

The result has large features with smaller ones riding on them, the way real ground does. Every other field on this page is noise like this: the plain's rolling, the wobble of the rim, how wide each cliff is, how ragged its edge is.

Ingredient two, and the main answer

The bent ruler

Nearly every shape here is defined by a distance. The cone's height depends on how far a column is from the centre. The caldera wall sits at a set distance from it, and so does the island. Measure those distances with a straight ruler and you get perfect circles: the right-hand render at the top.

So the distances are measured with a bent ruler instead. Before measuring, every column's coordinates are pushed sideways by two noise fields: a big slow one up to 12 blocks, which bends the whole outline, and a small fast one up to 4 blocks, which wobbles its edge. Then the same formulas run unchanged. A circle measured this way comes out as a lumpy, pinched, bulging loop, and the cliffs, gullies and island all inherit that shape for free.

Try it. With both sliders at zero the cone is a perfect cone. The black rings mark every 10 blocks of distance as the bent ruler measures it.

# the whole trick, in four lines
Xw = X + 12 * fbm(cell=90, seed=210) + 4 * fbm(cell=22, seed=212)
Zw = Z + 12 * fbm(cell=90, seed=211) + 4 * fbm(cell=22, seed=213)
d  = hypot(Xw - cx, Zw - cz)            # distance with the bent ruler
cone = 12 + 108 * (1 - d / 145) ** 1.35  # the same formula as ever
What the code actually wrote

Every shape in the recipe, as written and as measured

Every shape the recipe names, drawn by the kind of geometry it is written as, in the Riftwater page's key: pink for a circle or ellipse (a centre and radii), orange for a rectangle, purple for a line defined by noise, grey for a band. There is no blue, because this recipe has no line of points. The ground under both drawings is the finished caldera.

As written shows what the numbers in the code say: four clean ellipses, a box and thirty straight spokes. As measured shows the same shapes where the bent ruler actually puts them, and that difference is most of the detail. The ellipses turn into lumpy loops, and the spokes bend once the gully phase gets its noise. The finished ground drifts off the written shapes and sits exactly on the measured ones.

  1. Cone foot Ellipse at (0, 0), radius 145, squashed to 0.9 and turned 30°. Where the cone reaches the plain; smooth_max melts it 16 blocks further out.
  2. Caldera, first cliff Where the wall starts: 42 blocks out on an ellipse squashed to 0.82, plus a 10% wobble of its own. The cliff then rises over 6 to 12 blocks, depending on the width field.
  3. Caldera, the bench's edge 58 blocks out, where the second cliff starts climbing to the outer slope.
  4. Island Ellipse at (−18, 14), radius 17, squashed to 0.75 and turned 70°, 26 blocks high.
  5. Island crater Radius 4 on the same ellipse, 5 blocks deep.
  6. Slump box Rectangle (−50, −45) to (50, 45): 26 blocks are tried at random inside it. Only those that land between the faint pink rings (30 to 48 out) are kept: of them, drawn as small ellipses.
  7. Notch wedge A band by angle, 2.3 radians (about 132°, south-west) ± 0.25, between 30 and 80 blocks out. The rim drops up to 14 blocks inside it.
  8. Gully grooves 30 lines where the groove's phase crosses a whole number, only between 70 and 135 blocks out (the grey rings). Written, the phase is the angle alone and the grooves are spokes. Measured, 0.9 of noise is added and they wander.

Why the crater wall looks like that

One cross-section, stretched differently everywhere

The wall is a single operation, landform.profile. It describes a cross-section by distance from the centre: the floor at y 30 out to 42 blocks, a cliff up to a bench at y 58, then a second cliff up to the outer slope. Every number in that description is allowed to be a whole field instead of a number, so the cross-section can be different in every column.

Four fields feed it. The distance itself is bent, plus a 10% wobble of its own. Each cliff's width runs from 6 blocks (near-sheer) to 12 (a steep slope), changing every 60 blocks or so around the ring. A jag field of up to ±3 blocks shifts where every cliff starts and ends, which gives the bitten, ragged edge. The floor rises and falls 4 blocks. The chart shows the result: the same wall, cut outward from the centre in eight directions.

H = profile(H, d=dc, floor=30, floor_ground=floor,
            steps=[Step(42, 42 + width, 58),
                   Step(52 + 0.6 * width, 58 + width, "ground")],
            jag=jag, talus=(6, 9), mode="cut")

The recipe, whole

Twenty lines

def caldera_lake(X, Z):
H = ground(X, 3)                                          # rolling plain: noise 90 and 25 across
Xw, Zw = warp(X, Z, 210, big=12, small=4)                 # the bent ruler
d0 = r_at(Xw, Zw, (0, 0), 0.9, 30)                        # distance, as a slightly turned ellipse
body = 12 + 108 * clip(1 - d0 / 145, 0, 1) ** 1.35 - gullies(Xw, Zw, (0, 0), 30, 6, 70, 135, seed=8)
H = smooth_max(H, body, 16)                               # the cone, with a 16-block foot
theta = arctan2(Zw, Xw)
H -= 14 * exp(-(angle(theta - 2.3) / 0.25) ** 2) * smoothstep(80, 55, d0) * smoothstep(30, 55, d0)   # notch
dc = r_at(Xw, Zw, (0, 0), 0.82, 30) * (1 + 0.1 * fbm(40, seed=9))
width = 6 + 6 * (0.5 + 0.5 * fbm(60, seed=11))
jag = 3 * fbm(9, seed=10)
floor = 30 + 4 * fbm(30, seed=12)
H = profile(H, dc, floor=30, floor_ground=floor,
            steps=[Step(42, 42 + width, 58), Step(52 + 0.6 * width, 58 + width, "ground")],
            jag=jag, talus=(6, 9), mode="cut")                # the caldera
H = lumps(H, X, Z, 26, (-50, -45, 50, 45), (3, 8), (3, 8), seed=13, mask=(dc < 48) & (dc > 30))
ir = r_at(Xw, Zw, (-18, 14), 0.75, 70)
H = smooth_max(H, where(ir < 17, 30 + 26 * clip(1 - ir / 17, 0, 1) ** 0.95, -inf), 4)   # island
H = crater_in(H, ir, 5, 4, 1, 1.3, k=2)                   # its crater
return H
