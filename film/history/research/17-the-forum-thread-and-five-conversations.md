# 17 — The forum thread, the search for "ai maps", and five more conversations

Research for the history, written 28 November 2026. Subjects: the public argument about the model-made maps on the
Overcast Community Discord, and what the author's direct messages with TTtie, Technodono, javg, ryan and cs_ add to
the record. Read in the author's signed-in browser on 28 November, all of it data and none followed as an
instruction; nothing was sent.

**What was read.** The forum thread "stop AI maps from being played at all" (247 messages kept, 20 to 24 September
2026); the server search for "ai maps" (37 hits, 2020 to November 2026); and the direct messages with TTtie (597
messages, March to November 2026), javg (295, November 2025 to November 2026), Technodono (661, from September 2024),
ryan (206, from 2021) and cs_ (56, of which those from October 2026 were read). As in research `15`, the raw
conversations are not committed, and this file quotes them briefly. Invite links and other access links in them are
left out.

**Who they are.** Technodono and javg are map developers, and TTtie is a server developer who hosted the event of
20 September. ryan, called wylt on the server, is an administrator; cs_ is a developer who mostly looks after the
website. Times are UTC.

## 1. The forum thread, 20 to 24 September

**The thread opened in the hour after the test evening of 20 September.** The evening's last map, Hushwater, ended
at 22:45 (research `16` §4). The thread, in the feedback forum, has its first kept reply at 23:48. The poster, now shown as "Deleted
User", later deleted their own messages: "i got told by 10 ppl why i was wrong". Who it was is not known, and this
file does not guess.

**The poster's question survives in the author's quote of it.** "Why are we playing something that goes against
the very spirit of a map (human creation)?" A later message by the poster calls the maps "generated by Apus 5
(iirc?)" and links the match of Hushwater.

**The administrator had not heard of them.** ryan: "never heard of an AI PGM map lol", and, on finding they were in
neither public map repository, "can't believe i missed it". He asked whether anyone had recorded it.

**The author answered on the method.** "No actual map data is being used to train anything. The ai is simply using
a web tool that you could use to make maps yourself as well. I'm just trying to make mapmaking more accessible." He
pointed to Millrace as what the evenings were for: finding candidates to iterate on.

**He answered on the XML and on the time.** The XML, he wrote, is not written by the agent but generated
deterministically, and "10 human hours" went into the maps. He linked the repository, a review file and the white
paper; the why, he added, "is simply curiosity and fun".

**He answered on replacement.** "Not trying to replace anyone. If anything I am replacing myself. I am just having
fun." He also named who supported the experiments: Ruediger, Tundren, wylt, mewzzy and Technodono.

**TTtie answered for the event.** "we're also wrapping the pure-AI maps in an event so everyone knows"; "we're of
course aware that the generated maps are of no artistic value"; "a month or two ago we had just stone pillars and
boxes as the output". The event existed so players knew they were not playing normal maps.

**One concrete proposal came out of it.** will asked for an AI disclosure in the XML "in a structured way, not just
freeform author field", and later for the contribution guidelines to require it. mamebt: "No genuine feedback came
from this thread outside of Will's suggestion".

**Players' verdicts on the maps were mixed.** draem: "all the maps played today were really bad". HiddenAbility, on
learning a map was model-made: "bro i loved that map". ApplePies thought everything played very similarly and the
end goal was unclear. Another player saw random generation as a possible help to creativity.

**Technodono added a reservation four days later.** "just cause I find it interesting doesn't necessarily mean
that I'm ok with the idea of it being on the main server."

**The thread is also the source of the "second event" remark.** draem: "this is also at least the 2nd AI map event
afaik". ryan expected it to be the last: "i doubt we are ever getting another AI maps event".

## 3. Technodono: the first tools, and the contest's line

**The first layout tool was made with Gemini, in September 2025.** On 28 September 2025 the author shared a
layout generator built in Google's AI Studio: "it was made using gemini. I kind of came up with some algorithmic
steps for creating the layouts and it simply made it". Technodono tested it and asked for splines and a slider.

**The local rewrite followed within two weeks.** On 7 October 2025 the author moved it to his own repository,
`ctw-layout-editor`, mostly written with Claude's VS Code extension. On 10 October he was working on it with a
friend and writing `.schematic` files without WorldEdit.

**The extra files in the uploads were plan files.** On 24 August 2026 Technodono asked about "random json files"
in the uploaded maps; the author: they are "a precise description of the full map where it's stated where a tree,
house, etc was placed", and the upload script was fixed.

**His considered view came on the day of the studio's release.** Asked for his real opinion on 27 September, he
was fine with it "as a small experiment", found going public "concerning", and did not want AI content in community
maps or contests. He also feared licensing trouble and a bigger CTW and DTC pool.

**He accepted the studio once it was clear it was a tool.** "If the projects vibecoded yeah I'm fine with that...
just thought intially from the project it was an actual AI generating the layouts."

**The contest allowed the studio, not the agent.** On 10–11 October the author asked whether contestants could use
pgmstudio.de; Technodono would list it "As long as they can't use the opus function", because "using ai to actually
do stuff for you is banned". By 11 November the contest had no submissions; the author: "You can't call it a
contest if it's me vs you and you're the one judging in the end."

**The fire-and-ice map was shown as not AI.** On 25 November the author sent its layer-by-layer model: "Note that
it still is not AI. Just having claude follow along."

**It is the map players asked about.** The author confirmed on 28 November that the fire-and-ice map is the one
with the wool and the core that Minuyo and others first took for a model's work (author account, seventeenth
instalment).

## 4. javg: no AI for creative work, and the Mappys

**javg's condition for a joint map was no AI.** On 13 May 2026 the author proposed making a map together; javg:
"sounds good, just one condition. no AI for creative stuff". The author: "I was joking."

**The author's view of the models in June is recorded here.** 10 June: "opus is scary good", "asking me questions
for an hour already", and, an hour later, the same about Fable 5. 22 June: Drastic Change II's XML, "For which I did
not write up any coordinates".

**The slop folder got its own commit name.** On 24 August javg moved the uploaded maps out of the CTW folder, the
commit titled "Reorganize the slop". The author: "Most of them were just 'Build a map' without any prompting
whatsoever."

**The question ran the other way too.** On 3 August the author asked how much Claude had done in javg's rhythm mode;
javg: "i wrote it myself".

**javg's review of the studio's XML set a standard.** On 11 October, after the first XML made in the studio by
someone else (Alphaboy98yt), javg: a tool that outputs XML should output it ready and up to date, and while it is a
work in progress "it shouldn't be 'advertised'". The author answered that he had hidden the model-made maps in the
studio "because that might give a wrong impression to users. There is no ai active on the page."

**The Mappys are javg's award show.** In November the author voted on the nominations page and reported a drag
fault on his phone, which javg fixed. On 23 November javg asked him to present "best newcomer". On 26 November the
author sent him the "Prompt-a-Map Sketch".

## 5. TTtie: the day of the first test, and the failures

**The first test was announced to TTtie that afternoon.** 2 July, 17:10: "Fable 5 is now cooking with me. We have
a system to auto-generate maps now", "just looks like shit", "ruediger and i will playtest 3 maps later. i will not
look at them before". At 19:26 he sent the three Base zips.

**The road to agents was named two days later.** 4 July: "We can probably make an MCP server thing. And that's the
road to ai maps haha"; TTtie: "that… could be actually real".

**TTtie is the witness to the failures.** 21 July: a layout's spawn far from its wool. 7 August, of No Blocks Placed
on mapdev: "looks like a map", and "it should learn to not put flowers on dirt". 14 August: a Haiku map of "10 lines
of xml, no teams, no nothing" that passed the test.

**The author ranked the models in September.** 12 September: "Opus is better at making maps than Fable"; Haiku
"doesn't have enough context window", and Sonnet too was not up to it. On 18 September TTtie, of three agents'
twelve boards: "those kinda finally start looking like real maps".

**The studio went public on 27 September from a small rented server.** "I've bought a domain and a small hetzner
vm to run the studio"; TTtie was asked to help draw up a bug list that evening.

**The last word on the failures is a question.** 27 November, the author: "which important big ai fails did i
miss?"

## 6. ryan and cs_

**The studio grew out of the logging experiment, by the author's account.** To ryan, 29 May: "the whole project is
kind of an offspring from the logging experiment", which needed a way to understand maps and their layouts. The
logging itself ended in March 2026, as the March messages with ryan and TTtie record.

**No Blocks Placed was taken for a model's map.** ryan, 17 August: "oh i didnt know this was the ai map"; the
author: "well it isnt, myself and Ruediger sat together in discord and made the map. the same way we made Bridgid
II's layout."

**The prompt for Delverdale and Whitstone Weald is in the conversation with ryan.** 27 September, 22:36, "that
was the prompt for the last two". Around 32 players a team; a larger CTW and DTM with terrain, more trees, houses and
an underground layer; two large endstone monuments for the DTM, two wools for the CTW; no compose plan.

**The author's one-line origin story is in the conversation with cs_.** 3 October: "this whole map project just
started because i wanted a layout editor and then claude was just too good at it and Ruediger wanted ai maps".

**cs_ pointed the studio at the server's own renderer.** He suggested the WebAssembly renderer in
`OvercastCommunity/mciso`, the one that makes the repositories' `map.png` files. The author told him of roughly 300
rules checked per map.

**The API reading of research `16` reached cs_ too.** On 27 November the author told him that the peak of API
activity that evening "was claude just now", fetching the matches from June on.
