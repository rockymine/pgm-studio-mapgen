# Opus 5.5 — collaboration run 1: two boards with the author, three nano boards alone

## What I set out to build

Two capture boards decided stage by stage with the author — **Millbank**, a mill valley across a river gorge,
and **Clasp**, a clamp-wool board for a cold taiga — and then, alone overnight, three **nano** capture boards
prepared for theming: **Redcut** (a mesa, warm), **Lymedown** (chalk downs, pale) and **Sedgefall** (a fen,
dark). Every one stops before paint: plan and ground built, walkable, exported, grey.

The method was the author's: build the ground unpainted first, sketch it more than one way, combine what each
did well, and correct it one measured change at a time. Each change was read back with a transect or a
column before it was called done.

## What I could not say

**Nothing was missing from the system this run.** Every instrument asked for existed and was found:
footprints on room placements, a scarp's side from its tracing order, negative pushes, vertex edits on a
compiled subtract, per-vertex line-mark heights.

**One rule is narrower than the ruling it states.** `PL17` compares only the two pieces a wall names, so a
wall where an arm meets a wider hub passed while the hub ran on past both ends. Filed as `pgm-studio`
`TN22`, with the plan that showed it.

**One mark stands an artifact nothing reports.** A scarp traced round a corner stands a one-block ridge of its
high side past the corner, and the relief read names no seam because it is one mark. Written into
`GENERATION-NOTES.md`; one mark per run is the answer.

**Not tried: a relief stair's treads as stair blocks.** The 45-degree stairs are line marks, so they are
ground; a flight shape with a `material` could state stone treads, and that is a paint-stage choice.

## What I got wrong

**The first holm came out flat.** A half-turn solves a group straddling the axis on its `z < 0` half, and I
had stated every mark on the other half. Nothing warned; the relief read listed no silent mark.

**A push drawn wholly over the void lifted nothing.** Its falloff is measured across the land, so a ring with
no land inside it moves no cell. It has to straddle the edge it follows.

**Walls went on the hub's side first.** Both boards' first walls sat where an arm met the hub, and the hub
ran on past their ends; the author caught it and a neck fixed it. `PL17` said nothing.

**A chamfer bent a room.** A corner four blocks from a wool room, cut back three, bent the outline at the room
itself. Chamfers now stop twelve blocks or more from a room.

**A level mark reached past its pieces.** The mark holding Clasp's clamp at 20 overlapped two blocks of the hub
where the scarp pinned its foot at 12, and stood an eight-block step there. `RL3` named it and the mark was
trimmed to the clamp.

**I said a finding was undocumented when it was.** The notes already carried the half-solve entry; my search
pattern missed it. The entry was sharpened instead of duplicated.

## What worked first time

**Four reliefs side by side, unpainted.** The author picked one in a round and named what to take from the
others.

**Transects as the proof of every claim.** A stair is "walked end to end, worst step 1" or it is not.

**The neck.** Once named, every later wall went behind one and none was flanked.

**Stairs as line marks from a scarp's foot**, a block of rise per block of run; they walked on every board.

**Sedgefall's balance.** Carrying the side wool a cell further passed both balance rules at once.

| Redcut's crossing has no island: keep it open? | Kept open, 32 blocks, so the three nano boards cross three different ways |

| The three settings | Redcut warm (badlands), Lymedown pale, Sedgefall dark (swampland) — so five boards are not five greens |
